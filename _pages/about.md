I am a Biological Oceanographer studying planktonic distributions and interactions across trophic, spatial, and temporal scales. For my MSc thesis under the direction of <a href="https://colsa.unh.edu/person/elizabeth-harvey" target="_blank"> Dr. Elizabeth Harvey</a>, I quantified Transparent Exopolymer Particles (TEP, basically polysaccharide dominated 'sticky' colloidal organic matter) using Spectrophotometry during a mesocosm experiment in Norway (MesoHux 2017) as well as across the estuary-gradient of the South Atlantic Bight shelf. In my thesis, I also examined TEP accumulation rates in the context of dilution experiments and microzooplankton grazing rates. As a PhD student under the guidance of <a href="https://www.skio.uga.edu/people/faculty/adam-greer/" target="_blank"> Dr. Adam Greer</a>, I contributed significant amounts to development and image-validation processing of a modular shadowgraph imaging platform, developed by <a href="https://bellamare-us.com/" target="_blank">Bellamare LLC</a> that resulted in co-authorship of a methods manuscript in <a href="https://aslopubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/lom3.10657" target="_blank" >*Limnology & Oceanography: Methods*</a>. For my dissertation research, I used shadowgraph *in situ* imaging to understand how<a href="https://aslopubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/lno.70458" target="_blank" > life stages of cosmopolitan *blooming* gelatinous zooplankton, the pelagic tunicates salps and doliolids, occupy different realized niche space under the same available habitat conditions</a>. In my dissertation, I also used the fine-scale observations of an imaging vehicle to correlate the vertical spatial layering of doliolid populations with the vertical layering of Chlorophyll-*a* fluorescence in a Western Boundary Current region, and to understand how zooplankton functional traits occupy vertical water column space in a seasonally productive shelf. Across my MSc and PhD field experiences, I have gained skills to quantify in situ abundances of organic materials from colloids to macrozooplankton using Spectrophotometry, FlowCAM, Shadowgraph imaging, and traditional net methods; this includes using machine learning approaches to differentiate living and detrital material in image data. 

My published authored and co-authored research spans ~6 orders of magnitude in organism size, from phytoplankton-grazing dynamics to macrozooplankton ecology. From my <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10236244.2020.1713701" target="_blank" >undergraduate research in using cameras and CellTrak to quantify *Artemia* response to light cues under kairomone </a> (a fish mucous molecule potentially sensed by zooplankton) concentration gradients I have been inspired with the use of imaging to quantify biological processes. I have an interdisciplinary skillset that combines field observations, experimental incubations and rates, existing datasets, and big-data computational approaches. My primary research interests are to better understand gelatinous zooplankton biogeochemical roles and to integrate observations from different observational platforms (in situ imaging + acoustics, satellites, eDNA, and more) with rate-measurements to pursue novel insights into ocean functions.
